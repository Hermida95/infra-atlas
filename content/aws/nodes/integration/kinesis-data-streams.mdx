A stream is an append-only log that many consumers read independently, each tracking its own
position. That is the structural difference from a queue: in SQS a message is deleted once
handled, so adding a second consumer means adding a second queue; in Kinesis the data stays for
the retention period and every consumer sees all of it, which also makes **replay** possible.

**Shards** are the capacity unit in provisioned mode -- 1 MB/s or 1,000 records per second in, 2
MB/s out, shared across consumers. Ordering is guaranteed **within a shard**, and a record's
**partition key** chooses the shard, so the key is simultaneously your sharding strategy and
your ordering group. A key with poor cardinality produces a hot shard that throttles while the
others idle, which is the same failure mode as DynamoDB and has the same fix. **On-demand mode**
removes the sizing question entirely and is usually the right starting point.

When several consumers share a shard's 2 MB/s they compete, and `GetRecords` polling adds
latency. **Enhanced fan-out** gives each registered consumer its own 2 MB/s per shard with push
delivery over HTTP/2, at around 70 ms. It costs more per consumer and is the answer when either
the consumer count or the latency requirement rules out sharing.

What it is for: clickstreams, application and IoT telemetry, log aggregation, change feeds, and
anything where several teams want the same data for different purposes. **Firehose** is the
no-code consumer that lands it in S3, Redshift or OpenSearch; **Managed Service for Apache
Flink** is the one for windowed aggregation and stream joins; **Lambda** is the one for
per-record processing, one concurrent invocation per shard.

Against the alternatives: **SQS** when each message has exactly one handler and order barely
matters -- it is simpler and cheaper. **Kinesis** when ordering, replay or multiple independent
consumers matter. **MSK** when the requirement names Kafka, its ecosystem or an existing Kafka
estate.
