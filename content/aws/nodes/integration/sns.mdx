SNS is publish-subscribe: a publisher sends to a **topic**, and every subscriber gets a copy.
The publisher knows nothing about who is listening, which is what makes adding a consumer a
zero-change operation on the producing side.

Subscribers can be SQS queues, Lambda functions, HTTPS endpoints, email, SMS, mobile push, Data
Firehose or an event bus. **Topic types** mirror SQS: standard for unlimited throughput and
best-effort ordering, FIFO for strict ordering and deduplication -- and a FIFO topic can only
deliver to FIFO queues.

The pattern worth naming is **fan-out**: SNS in front of several SQS queues. Each consumer gets
its own queue, its own backlog, its own retry behaviour and its own dead-letter queue, so one
slow or broken consumer does not affect the others. SNS straight to Lambda is simpler and has no
buffer: a throttled or failing function loses the delivery after the retry policy is exhausted
unless a dead-letter queue is attached. For anything that must not be lost, put a queue in
between.

**Message filtering** is what stops every subscriber receiving everything. A subscription filter
policy matches on message attributes or on the message body, so one topic serves many consumers
each taking only their slice, instead of a topic per event type.

Other details that come up: **message delivery retries** are built in with an exponential policy
and per-subscription dead-letter queues; **encryption** uses KMS and the key policy must allow
the publishing service; and **cross-account** publishing and subscribing is a topic policy,
which is a resource-based policy like any other.

Against EventBridge: SNS is fan-out to subscribers you register, with high throughput and low
latency. EventBridge routes on content with a schema registry, archive and replay, and
integrates with hundreds of SaaS and AWS event sources. For simple broadcast, SNS. For
event-driven architecture with many producers and routing rules, EventBridge.
