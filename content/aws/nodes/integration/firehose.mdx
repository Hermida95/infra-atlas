Data Firehose is the managed path from a stream to storage. There are no shards and no consumer
to write: you point a delivery stream at a destination, and it buffers, transforms, compresses,
encrypts and delivers, retrying on its own.

**Buffering** is the central idea: it accumulates until a size (1-128 MB) or a time (60-900
seconds) threshold is reached, then writes one object. That is what makes it near-real-time
rather than real-time -- a minute of latency is the normal price -- and it is also what makes
the output usable, because a million tiny files in S3 is the thing that makes Athena slow and
expensive.

The transformations that matter happen on the way through. **Format conversion** to Parquet or
ORC turns JSON into a columnar format, which is where most of the downstream query saving comes
from. **Dynamic partitioning** writes to `s3://bucket/year=2026/month=11/day=05/` based on
fields in the record, so Athena prunes partitions instead of scanning everything. A **Lambda
transform** can enrich, filter or reshape records in flight, with failures routed to a separate
S3 prefix rather than dropped.

Sources are Kinesis Data Streams, MSK, or direct `PutRecord` calls -- and a great deal of AWS
plumbing uses it: CloudWatch Logs subscription filters, VPC flow logs, WAF logs and EventBridge
can all deliver through Firehose. Destinations are S3, Redshift, OpenSearch, Splunk, Iceberg
tables and generic HTTP endpoints.

The decision against Kinesis Data Streams is simple: if you need custom processing, replay or
several independent consumers, you need a stream. If you need the data in S3 or a warehouse with
no code, Firehose is the whole answer.
