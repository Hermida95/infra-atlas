MSK is managed Apache Kafka: AWS runs the brokers and, in current versions, the coordination
layer, handling patching, broker replacement and multi-AZ placement. The API is Kafka, so
existing producers, consumers, Kafka Connect connectors and Kafka Streams applications work
unchanged.

That compatibility is the entire reason to choose it over Kinesis. An organization with Kafka
expertise, existing Connect pipelines, Schema Registry, Streams applications or a hybrid
deployment that must mirror between data centres and AWS gets to keep all of it. Kafka also
offers things Kinesis does not: log compaction, consumer groups with their own rebalancing
semantics, and arbitrary retention and partition counts.

**MSK Serverless** removes capacity planning -- no brokers to size, automatic partition scaling,
pay per hour and per GB -- at a higher unit cost, which suits variable or unpredictable
workloads. Provisioned clusters are cheaper at steady high volume and give control over broker
type, storage and tiered storage for long retention.

The honest comparison with Kinesis Data Streams: Kinesis is simpler, cheaper for most volumes,
has no brokers at all and integrates natively with Lambda, Firehose and the rest of AWS. MSK is
more work and more capable, and is the right answer when Kafka itself is the requirement. For a
greenfield AWS workload with no Kafka history, Kinesis is usually the better default, and exams
tend to phrase the MSK case explicitly as "the team already uses Kafka" or "must use the Kafka
API".
