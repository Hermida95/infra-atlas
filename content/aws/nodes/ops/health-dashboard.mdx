AWS Health is the feed of events that affect **your** resources, which is what separates it from
the public status page. The Service Health Dashboard says a service is degraded in a region;
Health says that *your* instance is on hardware scheduled for retirement next Tuesday, that
*your* certificate expires in thirty days, or that *your* RDS instance will be patched in the
next maintenance window.

Three kinds of event: **issues** (a service problem), **scheduled changes** (retirements,
maintenance, deprecations) and **account notifications**. The **Organizational View** aggregates
them for every account from the management account, which is the only practical way to see them
across an estate.

The reason to care operationally is that these are **EventBridge events**. An EC2 instance
retirement notice can automatically open a ticket, or trigger an Auto Scaling instance refresh
that replaces the instance before the deadline, with no human reading an email. The same is true
for certificate expiry, EBS volume degradation and service deprecations.

The common failure mode is that these notices go to the account's root email address, which
nobody reads. Routing them through EventBridge to the team's actual alerting is a small piece of
setup that prevents a whole category of avoidable incident.
