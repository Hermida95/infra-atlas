Trusted Advisor runs checks against your account in five categories -- cost optimisation,
performance, security, fault tolerance and service limits -- and tells you what to do about
them.

The support plan decides how much you get. On Basic and Developer you get a handful of core
checks: service limits and some security basics. The **full set requires Business, Enterprise
On-Ramp or Enterprise** support, and that is the detail exam questions hinge on.

What it is genuinely good at: **idle and underused resources** (unattached EBS volumes, idle
load balancers, low-utilisation instances, unassociated Elastic IPs) that quietly cost money;
**service limits** approaching their quota, which is the early warning before a launch fails;
**security basics** such as open security groups, public snapshots, exposed access keys and
missing MFA on root; and **fault tolerance** gaps such as a single-AZ Auto Scaling group or an
RDS instance without Multi-AZ.

Where it fits among the overlapping tools: **Compute Optimizer** gives better right-sizing
recommendations because it reads actual utilisation metrics; **Cost Explorer** and **Budgets**
own the money; **Security Hub** owns security findings at depth. Trusted Advisor is the broad,
low-effort sweep that catches the obvious, and its checks are available through EventBridge and
the API so they can feed a weekly report instead of waiting for someone to open the console.
