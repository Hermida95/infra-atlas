Batch is a job queue and a scheduler for containerised work that runs to completion. You submit
jobs; it decides what capacity is needed, launches it, runs them, and tears the capacity down.
The reason it exists is that this is precisely the workload Spot is best at, and doing it by
hand is a lot of code.

Four objects. A **compute environment** is where jobs can run -- managed Fargate, managed EC2,
or EC2 you manage -- with the instance types and the Spot or on-demand mix. A **job queue**
feeds one or more compute environments in priority order, which is how you say "try Spot first,
fall back to on-demand". A **job definition** is the container, vCPU, memory, role and retry
policy. A **job** is one submission, and it can declare dependencies on others, so a pipeline is
expressible without an orchestrator.

The features that matter in practice: **array jobs** for ten thousand near-identical tasks
indexed by a variable, **retry strategies** that can specifically retry a Spot interruption
without burning an attempt on a genuine failure, **fair-share scheduling** so one team's
submission does not starve everyone else, and **multi-node parallel** jobs for tightly coupled
MPI work.

Where it sits against the alternatives: Lambda for work that finishes in under fifteen minutes,
Batch for long CPU-bound jobs where cost matters, Step Functions to orchestrate a pipeline whose
heavy steps are Batch jobs, and EMR when the job is Spark or Hive rather than a container of
your own.
