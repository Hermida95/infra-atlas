An Auto Scaling group turns individual instances into capacity. It keeps a desired count
running, spreads it across the zones you list, replaces anything that fails a health check, and
moves the count up and down. The moment a design has a group in it, instances stop being things
you log into and start being things that are replaced -- which is also why the AMI and the user
data matter more than they did.

**Target tracking** is the policy to reach for first: name a metric and a target -- average CPU
at 50%, or `ALBRequestCountPerTarget` at 1000 -- and the group does the arithmetic, creating the
alarms itself. Request count per target is usually the better signal, because CPU is a symptom
and arrives late. **Step scaling** is for when you want explicit bands. **Scheduled** is for a
shape you already know, like a working day. **Predictive** reads fourteen days of history and
provisions ahead of a daily pattern, which is the answer when scale-up is slower than the ramp.

Health checks are where most real incidents hide. The default only checks that EC2 says the
instance is running, so an application that has crashed inside a healthy instance stays in
service forever. Turn on **ELB health checks** so the group replaces anything the load balancer
will not send traffic to, and set the **grace period** longer than a real boot or the group
kills instances while they are still starting -- a loop that looks like a scaling storm.

Two things that make the difference between a group that works and one that drops requests.
**Connection draining** (deregistration delay on the target group) lets in-flight requests
finish before an instance goes away. And **lifecycle hooks** pause an instance in `Pending:Wait`
or `Terminating:Wait` so something can run -- warm a cache, pull secrets, upload logs -- before
it serves or disappears.

For cost, a **mixed instances policy** puts on-demand and Spot in one group with a list of
acceptable types and a floor of on-demand capacity you are not willing to lose. Combined with
**capacity rebalancing**, which replaces a Spot instance when AWS signals it is at risk rather
than waiting for the two-minute notice, that is most of the saving with little of the pain. And
**warm pools** keep pre-initialised instances stopped and ready, for applications whose boot is
measured in minutes.
