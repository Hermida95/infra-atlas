EC2 is a virtual machine: you choose the hardware shape, the image, the network and the storage,
and everything above the hypervisor is yours to run and patch. It is the most flexible compute
on AWS and the most work, and every other compute service is a trade of some of that flexibility
for some of that work.

**The instance type** is the first decision and most of the answer: the family letter is the
workload's bottleneck (`m` balanced, `c` compute, `r` memory, `i` storage, `g`/`p`
accelerators), the generation should be the newest available, and **Graviton** is typically 20%
cheaper and often faster for anything that can run on Arm.

**Storage** splits three ways. The **EBS** root volume survives a stop and is zonal. Extra EBS
volumes are the same. **Instance store** is physically attached NVMe -- the fastest thing
available, and gone the moment the instance stops. The rule is that anything you would miss
lives on EBS or S3.

**Identity** is an **instance profile** carrying an IAM role, and there is no acceptable
alternative. The credentials appear at the instance metadata endpoint, which is why **IMDSv2**
must be enforced: with IMDSv1, a server-side request forgery bug in your application reads the
role's credentials with a single GET, and that is the most common way an EC2 role gets stolen.

**Access** should be **Session Manager**, not SSH: a shell through IAM, with no key pairs to
distribute, no bastion host, no inbound port 22, and a full session log. For a private subnet
with no NAT it needs the `ssm`, `ssmmessages` and `ec2messages` endpoints.

**The lifecycle** is the mental shift. An instance you log into and fix is a pet; in an **Auto
Scaling group** it is capacity, replaced rather than repaired, launched from a **launch
template** that points at a baked **AMI**. That is also what makes the purchase model a design
decision -- on-demand for what cannot be interrupted, **Spot** at up to 90% off for what can,
and a **Savings Plan** for the steady baseline.

What it still wins at: anything needing the operating system, a specific kernel, a GPU, a
per-socket licence, a long-running process, or software that simply has not been containerised.
Everything else is usually cheaper and quieter on Fargate or Lambda.
