An endpoint keeps traffic to AWS services inside the AWS network. No Internet Gateway, no NAT
Gateway, no public addresses, and nothing that leaves through the internet. Getting the two
kinds straight is the single most useful piece of VPC knowledge for both the exam and the bill.

A **gateway endpoint** exists for exactly two services, S3 and DynamoDB. It is not a device: it
is an entry in a route table pointing at a managed prefix list, so traffic to those services is
routed away from the NAT Gateway. It costs nothing -- no hourly charge and no data processing --
which makes it the first fix for any workload moving serious volume to S3 from private subnets.
Two limits: it only works from inside the VPC, so on-premises traffic over Direct Connect cannot
use it, and it must be associated with every route table that needs it.

An **interface endpoint** is PrivateLink: an elastic network interface with a private address in
each subnet you choose. It works for nearly every AWS API, for SaaS partners, and for services
another account exposes. It is charged per endpoint per zone per hour plus per gigabyte -- still
cheaper than NAT above a modest volume, but three zones means paying three times for each
endpoint, so put them only where they are needed. Unlike gateway endpoints they **are**
reachable from on-premises over Direct Connect or VPN, which is often the real reason to choose
one.

**Private DNS** is why this is invisible to your code. With it enabled, `s3.eu-west-1.
amazonaws.com` resolves to the endpoint's private address inside the VPC, so the SDK is
unchanged. It needs `enableDnsSupport` and `enableDnsHostnames` on the VPC; without them the
endpoint exists and nothing uses it.

The security pattern worth memorising is the pair. An **endpoint policy** says which buckets may
be reached through this endpoint; a **bucket policy** with a `aws:SourceVpce` condition says
this bucket may only be reached through that endpoint. Together, credentials stolen from the
environment are useless from anywhere else, and data cannot be copied into a bucket outside the
list. That is the standard answer to any exam question containing the words "prevent data
exfiltration".

Services that need an endpoint more often than people expect: ECR (two of them, `api` and `dkr`,
plus S3 for the layers), Systems Manager (`ssm`, `ssmmessages`, `ec2messages` for Session
Manager), Secrets Manager, KMS and CloudWatch Logs.
