A peering connection joins two VPCs so their instances talk over private addresses. It works
across accounts and across regions, there is no bandwidth bottleneck or single point of failure
because there is no appliance, and traffic within a zone is free. For two VPCs it is the right
answer and costs almost nothing.

It has two properties that decide every design question about it. Peering is **non-transitive**:
if A peers with B and B peers with C, A cannot reach C, and no route table entry will make it.
And the CIDRs **must not overlap**, with no way around it. Together these mean the number of
connections grows as n(n-1)/2 -- three VPCs need three, ten need forty-five -- and every one of
them needs route table entries, security group references and DNS resolution configured on both
sides.

That is the point at which a **Transit Gateway** becomes the answer: one attachment per VPC,
routing in the middle, and segmentation through its route tables. The rough rule is that peering
is fine up to a handful of VPCs with a stable, mostly-pairwise pattern, and a Transit Gateway is
right as soon as the picture starts to look like a mesh. **PrivateLink** is the third option,
and the better one when the requirement is a single service rather than a network.

Two details worth carrying. Enable **DNS resolution** on the connection or the peer's private
hosted zone names will not resolve. And a security group in one VPC can reference a security
group in the peered VPC, which keeps the clean group-to-group pattern working across the
boundary -- in the same region only.
