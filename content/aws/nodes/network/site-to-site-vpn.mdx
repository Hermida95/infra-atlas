An IPsec tunnel between your network and AWS, over the public internet. It is up in minutes,
costs a small hourly rate plus data transfer, and is encrypted by definition -- which is the
thing Direct Connect is not.

Every connection comes with **two tunnels** terminating on different AWS endpoints, and both
should be configured. Many outages are a customer router with one tunnel set up and the other
left for later. On the AWS side it terminates on a **virtual private gateway** attached to one
VPC, or on a **Transit Gateway**, which is the better default because the same tunnel then
reaches every VPC behind it.

Routing is static or BGP, and BGP is worth the trouble: it fails over between tunnels without
anyone being paged, and with a Transit Gateway it supports **equal-cost multipath**, so several
tunnels aggregate rather than sitting idle. A single tunnel is capped around 1.25 Gbps, so ECMP
is also the way to get past that number.

Its two jobs in a design. As the **backup** for Direct Connect, with BGP preferring the circuit
and failing over to the tunnel automatically -- the standard resilient pattern, and the cheapest
way to stop a cable cut being an outage. And as the **immediate** option while a circuit is
provisioned, or for sites too small to justify one at all. **Accelerated VPN** routes the tunnel
over the AWS backbone from the nearest edge location, which takes some of the jitter out of the
internet portion.
