Aurora is a MySQL- and PostgreSQL-compatible engine on top of a storage layer AWS wrote. The
storage is the product. It keeps **six copies of each 10 GB segment across three Availability
Zones**, grows to 128 TB without anyone resizing anything, and is self-healing -- it can lose
two copies and still take writes, three and still serve reads. Because the storage is shared,
the database instances in front of it are nearly stateless, and that is where the features come
from.

Replicas read the same storage instead of replaying a replication log, so lag is typically
**under 100 milliseconds** and adding a replica does not add write load to the writer. Failover
is seconds, not minutes, because the new writer does not need to load anything. A **clone** of a
10 TB cluster takes minutes and almost no space, because it is copy-on-write -- which makes "a
full-size copy of production for this test" a reasonable request instead of a project.
**Backtrack** rewinds the whole cluster to a point in time in place, without a restore, which
turns a bad migration from an outage into a few minutes.

The endpoints matter in the application. The **writer endpoint** follows the current writer
through a failover. The **reader endpoint** round-robins across replicas. **Custom endpoints**
let you point reporting at the two large instances and the API at the small ones. Pointing
everything at the writer endpoint is the most common way to under-use an Aurora cluster.

Costs are instance hours, storage and **I/O**, and the I/O line surprises people on write-heavy
workloads -- **Aurora I/O-Optimized** swaps it for a higher instance price and is cheaper past
roughly 25% of the bill being I/O.

Against RDS: Aurora is faster, more available and usually cheaper per transaction at scale, at
the price of being AWS-specific and having a floor on cost. Against DynamoDB: Aurora is the
answer when you need joins, transactions across tables and SQL; DynamoDB is the answer when you
need single-digit milliseconds at any scale and can design around the access patterns.
