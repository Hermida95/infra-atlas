DynamoDB is a managed key-value and document store with single-digit millisecond reads at any
scale. There are no servers, no connections, no version upgrades and no capacity to plan if you
do not want any. What it asks in return is that you design for the access patterns before the
table exists, because there is no query planner to rescue a bad key choice.

**The model.** An item is up to 400 KB. The **partition key** is hashed to choose a partition;
the optional **sort key** orders items within it and enables range queries. Together they are
the item's identity. Anything the keys do not support becomes a `Scan`, which reads the whole
table -- and a Scan in production is almost always a design error rather than a tuning problem.

**Capacity.** **On-demand** charges per request and handles any traffic shape, which makes it
the right default for new or spiky workloads. **Provisioned** with auto scaling is cheaper for
predictable load. Either way, an uneven partition key produces a **hot partition** that
throttles while the rest of the table idles, and the fix is a better key or **write sharding**.

**Consistency and transactions.** Reads are eventually consistent by default and strongly
consistent on request at twice the cost -- and strongly consistent reads do not work through DAX
or against a global secondary index. `TransactWriteItems` gives ACID across up to 100 items in
one region, and **conditional writes** are how optimistic locking and idempotency are
implemented without a transaction.

**The features that come free and change designs.** **TTL** expires items at a timestamp with no
cost, which is how sessions and temporary data clean themselves up. **Streams** turn every
change into an event for Lambda. **Point-in-time recovery** gives any second in the last 35
days. **Global tables** replicate multi-writer across regions. **DAX** adds a microsecond read
cache that invalidates itself.

**Where it is the wrong answer:** ad-hoc queries, reporting and aggregations across the whole
table, anything needing joins, and access patterns that are not known in advance. Those are
relational or analytics problems, and forcing them into DynamoDB produces the scans it is
designed to avoid.
