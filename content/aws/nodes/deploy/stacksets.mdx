A StackSet deploys one CloudFormation template to many accounts and many regions from a single
place, and tracks the result of each instance.

The reason it exists is the baseline problem. Every account needs the same things: a Config
recorder, a GuardDuty detector, an IAM role for the security team, VPC flow logs, a standard set
of SCPable resources, the organisation's tags. Doing that by hand means it is right in the
accounts somebody remembered.

Two permission models. **Self-managed** requires an administration role in the management
account and an execution role in each target -- the older model, still needed outside an
organization. **Service-managed** uses Organizations directly, targets **OUs** rather than
account lists, and supports **automatic deployment**: an account created tomorrow, or moved into
the OU, receives the stack without anyone doing anything. That last property is the whole point,
and it is why targeting an OU rather than a list of ids is the rule.

Operationally, the controls that matter are the **deployment preferences**: maximum concurrent
accounts, failure tolerance, and region order. Rolling a change across two hundred accounts with
a failure tolerance of zero and concurrency of one is slow and safe; the opposite is fast and
occasionally memorable. **Drift detection** reports where someone has changed a stack instance
by hand.

Control Tower's customisations ride on StackSets, which is why the two usually appear together.
And the natural pairing in an exam answer is "SCPs for what must not happen, StackSets for what
must exist".
