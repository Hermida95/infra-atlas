SAM is a CloudFormation **transform**: a short dialect for serverless resources that expands
into plain CloudFormation at deploy time. `AWS::Serverless::Function` becomes a function, its
role, its log group, its permissions and its API route -- twenty lines instead of two hundred.

Because it is a transform, SAM and ordinary CloudFormation resources mix freely in one template,
which matters when the serverless part is only part of the stack.

The piece people actually use it for is **`sam local`**: invoking a function locally in a
container that matches the Lambda runtime, running the API Gateway locally, and generating
sample events for S3, SQS and the rest. That shortens the loop from deploy-and-watch-logs to
run-and-debug. **`sam sync`** goes further for development, pushing code changes to a deployed
function in seconds without a full CloudFormation update.

It also wires in the safe-deployment pieces by default: an `AutoPublishAlias` plus a
`DeploymentPreference` gives you CodeDeploy canary or linear traffic shifting on a Lambda alias,
with CloudWatch alarms as the rollback trigger, in a few lines of YAML.

Against CDK: SAM is declarative YAML, small, and specifically about serverless; CDK is a
general-purpose program. Teams building mostly-serverless applications often prefer SAM for its
simplicity and its local tooling; teams with mixed infrastructure usually end up in CDK or
Terraform, where the serverless parts are a smaller share of the whole.
