Cost Explorer is where the bill is broken down and where a spike gets a name. It slices by
service, account, region, instance type, usage type, purchase option and **tag**, over daily or
monthly granularity, with hourly and resource-level data available if you turn it on.

The practical loop: notice the number, group by service to find which one, group by usage type
to find what inside it, group by account and tag to find whose. Most surprises resolve in three
clicks and are one of a short list -- NAT Gateway data processing, cross-AZ transfer, idle load
balancers and Elastic IPs, orphaned EBS volumes and snapshots, CloudWatch Logs ingestion from
debug logging left on, or an S3 lifecycle rule nobody ever wrote.

Beyond the breakdown it does three useful things. **Forecasting** projects the month and the
quarter from current usage, which is what a budget threshold should be set against.
**Rightsizing recommendations** flag over-provisioned instances from real utilisation. **Savings
Plans recommendations** compute the commitment that matches your actual baseline, which is a
better basis than a guess.

The dependency to remember is **cost allocation tags**: they must be activated in the management
account, and they only populate from that point onward. Untagged resources appear as one
undifferentiated lump, which is why a tagging policy -- enforced with a Config rule and
remediation -- is a cost tool before it is a tidiness one.

Where it sits among the others: **Cost Explorer** explains the past and projects forward,
**Budgets** acts on thresholds, **Cost and Usage Report** is the raw line-item export for
anything more precise, **Compute Optimizer** recommends sizes, and **Trusted Advisor** sweeps
for the obvious waste.
