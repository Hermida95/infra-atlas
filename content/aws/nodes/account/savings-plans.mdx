Three commitment models, and choosing between them is mostly about how much flexibility you are
willing to trade for discount.

**Compute Savings Plans** commit to an hourly dollar spend for one or three years and apply
automatically to EC2 in **any** instance family, size, region, operating system and tenancy,
plus **Fargate and Lambda**. Up to about 66% off. This is the default recommendation, because it
survives a migration from EC2 to containers to serverless without being stranded.

**EC2 Instance Savings Plans** commit to a family in a region -- `m7` in eu-west-1 -- and go up
to about 72%, with size and OS flexibility inside that family. Choose it only for a baseline you
are confident will not move.

**Reserved Instances** are the older mechanism and mostly matter now for two things: services
Savings Plans do not cover (**RDS, ElastiCache, OpenSearch, Redshift, DynamoDB** reserved
capacity), and **zonal RIs**, which reserve capacity in a specific Availability Zone rather than
just discounting it.

Payment options -- no upfront, partial, all upfront -- step the discount up and the flexibility
down. One year, no upfront is the low-risk entry; three years, all upfront is the maximum bet.

How to decide: look at the **steady baseline** in Cost Explorer, commit to roughly 70-80% of it
rather than to the peak, and leave the variable top on demand or on Spot. Over-committing is the
expensive mistake, because an unused commitment is paid for anyway. Buy from the **management
account** so the discount pools across the organization, and set **coverage and utilisation
alerts** so a drift in either is noticed before the renewal.
