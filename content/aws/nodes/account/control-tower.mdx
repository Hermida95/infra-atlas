Control Tower is not a new service so much as an opinionated assembly of Organizations,
CloudTrail, Config, IAM Identity Center, Service Catalog and StackSets, set up the way AWS
recommends. The value is that a landing zone built by hand takes weeks and is subtly wrong in
ways that surface two years later.

What it creates: an organization with a **Security OU** containing a **log archive** account and
an **audit** account, an organization CloudTrail writing into the log archive, Config recording
everywhere and aggregating into audit, Identity Center for human access, and a set of
**controls**.

Controls come in three kinds and the distinction is worth knowing. **Preventive** controls are
SCPs -- the action is refused. **Detective** controls are Config rules -- the action happens and
is reported as non-compliant. **Proactive** controls are CloudFormation Hooks -- the resource is
blocked before it is created, during the deployment. They are grouped as mandatory (always on),
strongly recommended and elective.

**Account Factory** is the piece teams interact with: a Service Catalog product that provisions
an account with the baseline, the OU placement, the networking and the Identity Center
assignments already applied. An account becomes a request rather than a project, which is what
makes "one account per team per environment" practical.

Its limits are real. It is opinionated, and an existing estate that does not match its opinions
can be awkward to enrol -- though enrolling existing accounts and OUs is supported. Some regions
are not covered. And **drift** happens when people change things by hand underneath it, which
Control Tower detects but which still has to be repaired.

The honest alternative is building the same structure yourself with Organizations and StackSets,
which is the right choice when the opinions do not fit. Everyone else should start here, because
the thing Control Tower prevents is a landing zone that was never finished.
