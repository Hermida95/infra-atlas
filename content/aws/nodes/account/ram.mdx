Resource Access Manager shares a resource with other accounts instead of each account creating
its own. The resource stays owned, billed and managed by one account; the others simply use it.

The headline case is the **shared VPC**. A network account owns one well-designed VPC and shares
subnets with the workload accounts in an OU. Teams launch into subnets they do not own, the
platform team keeps control of routing, endpoints, NAT and inspection, and there is no mesh of
peering connections or a Transit Gateway bill to connect twenty VPCs that should have been one.
Security groups still work across the participants, and each account only sees its own
resources.

The second is a **Transit Gateway** shared with the organization, which is the normal way to run
one: a single gateway in the network account, attachments from every workload account, and no
per-account duplication.

What else can be shared, and comes up in exams: Route 53 Resolver rules (so every account
resolves on-premises names the same way), License Manager configurations, Aurora clusters for
read access, Outposts, Capacity Reservations, Image Builder components, and Lake Formation
catalogue permissions.

Two practical points. Sharing with an **OU or the whole organization** rather than with a list
of account ids is what keeps it working as accounts are created -- and it requires enabling
sharing within Organizations first. And the ownership split needs to be explicit: the owner
cannot see into the participant's resources, the participant cannot change the shared one, and
the billing for data transfer lands in places people do not expect until they have looked once.
