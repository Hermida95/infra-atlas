The Well-Architected Framework is a set of questions AWS asks about a workload, grouped into six
pillars, with design principles and best practices behind each. Most exam answers are one of
those principles in disguise, which is why reading the pillars is a surprisingly efficient way
to study.

The two that generate the most exam questions are **reliability** and **cost optimisation**, and
both reduce to a small number of habits: span Availability Zones, scale horizontally, automate
recovery, test it, stop guessing capacity, pay only for what you use, and attribute the spend so
somebody owns it.

The mechanism in practice is the **Well-Architected Tool** in the console: you register a
workload, answer the questions, and get a list of high and medium risk items with links to the
remediation. It is not a score and not a certification -- the output is a prioritised list of
things you already half knew, written down, which is its value.

Around it sit **lenses** for specific domains -- serverless, SaaS, machine learning, data
analytics, financial services, IoT -- each adding questions the general framework does not ask.
And **Trusted Advisor** is the automated slice of the same material run against your real
account, so the two are complementary: Trusted Advisor finds what a machine can see, the review
finds what only a conversation can.

The honest way to use it is as a recurring design review rather than a one-off audit, and to
accept that some risks are accepted deliberately. A workload with three documented, accepted
high risks is in better shape than one that has never been reviewed.
