Two tools under one name, and they solve opposite ends of the same problem.

**External access analysis** answers "what in this account can be reached from outside it". It
is not a scanner guessing at risk: it uses automated reasoning over the actual policies --
bucket policies, key policies, role trust policies, SQS, Lambda, ECR, EFS -- and proves which
resources are reachable by a principal outside the zone of trust you set, which is usually the
organization. Every finding is a real path, so there is nothing to triage away as a false
positive. Set the zone to the organization rather than the account, or every legitimate
cross-account share shows up as a finding.

**Policy generation** goes the other way. Point it at a role and it reads up to ninety days of
that role's CloudTrail history and writes the policy that covers exactly what was called,
resources included. That is least privilege reached by subtraction from evidence, instead of by
someone guessing which actions an SDK uses. The same service also validates a policy you wrote
against a hundred-plus checks, and **unused access analysis** flags roles, users and permissions
nobody has exercised in the window -- the pile that accumulates in every account and never gets
cleaned up because nobody can prove it is safe to delete.

Findings land in Security Hub with everything else, which is where they get worked rather than
admired one service at a time.
