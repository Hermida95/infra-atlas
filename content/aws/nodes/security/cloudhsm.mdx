CloudHSM is a dedicated, single-tenant hardware security module in your VPC, validated to **FIPS
140-3 Level 3**. You control it completely: AWS manages the hardware, the networking and the
backups, and has **no access to the keys** and no way to recover them. Lose your credentials and
the key material is gone, permanently.

That is the entire reason to choose it over KMS, and the requirement is usually written down
somewhere: a regulation demanding single-tenant hardware, a contract saying the cloud provider
must not be able to access key material, or an application that needs **PKCS#11**, **JCE** or
**CNG/KSP** interfaces rather than an AWS API.

What it costs is operations. It is a cluster of HSM instances you size, spread across zones and
pay for by the hour whether busy or not; you manage users, quorum policies and the client SDK;
and you are responsible for the availability design. KMS, by contrast, is multi-tenant,
regional, highly available by default, integrated with every AWS service, and priced per key and
per request.

The useful middle ground is the **KMS custom key store**: KMS fronts your CloudHSM cluster, so
services keep using KMS normally while the key material stays in hardware you alone control. You
get the integrations and the compliance claim at once, at the price of operating the cluster
underneath.

Typical workloads: certificate authorities and code signing, payment processing under PCI PIN or
P2PE, TDE for Oracle or SQL Server, and custom cryptographic operations the KMS API does not
expose.
