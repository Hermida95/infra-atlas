Macie answers a question nobody can answer by hand: **what sensitive data is sitting in our S3
buckets?** It uses managed identifiers for the obvious categories -- credit card numbers,
national identifiers, passport and driving licence numbers, AWS keys, private keys, health and
financial data -- and supports custom identifiers as regular expressions with keyword proximity
for whatever is specific to your business.

It has two modes, and the free one is the one people forget. **Bucket inventory and posture** is
continuous and covers every bucket in the account: which are public, which are unencrypted,
which are shared with other accounts, which are missing Block Public Access. That alone is worth
enabling. **Sensitive data discovery jobs** then sample or fully scan objects, and those are
charged per gigabyte, which is why the sensible pattern is to scope jobs by bucket, prefix or
tag rather than pointing it at a petabyte lake.

Findings go to Security Hub and EventBridge, so "a bucket that now contains personal data became
publicly readable" can be an automated response rather than a quarterly review.

Where it shows up in exam scenarios: GDPR or HIPAA scope discovery, a data classification
requirement before a migration, and the specific shape of "we think there may be customer data
in a bucket nobody owns". It is **S3-only** -- for databases and other stores the answer is a
different tool -- and that limitation is also where the forgotten CSV export usually is.
