Nearly all encryption at rest on AWS has the same shape, and KMS is at the centre of it. The
service holds key material that never leaves it in plaintext, performs cryptographic operations
on request, and logs every one of them to CloudTrail.

**Envelope encryption** is why a service that can only handle 4 KB can encrypt a 5 TB object.
The service asks KMS for a **data key**; KMS returns it twice, once in plaintext and once
encrypted under the KMS key. The service encrypts your data with the plaintext data key,
discards it from memory, and stores the encrypted copy alongside the data. To decrypt, it sends
the encrypted data key back to KMS. Your bytes never go to KMS, which is also why throughput is
not a function of object size.

The choice that matters is **AWS managed versus customer managed** keys. An AWS managed key
(`aws/s3`, `aws/ebs`) is free, rotates yearly, and you cannot see or change its policy. A
**customer managed key** costs a dollar a month plus requests and gives you three things: a
**key policy** you control, **CloudTrail visibility** of every decrypt (which is how you
discover who is reading what), and the ability to **disable or schedule deletion**, which is the
basis of cryptographic erasure. Any requirement mentioning audit, key control or revocation
means a customer managed key.

The **key policy** is unusual and worth a careful read: it is the resource policy of the key,
and unlike most services an IAM policy alone is never enough -- the key policy must also allow
the principal. This is the one place where a careless policy can lock out the account's own
administrator with no recovery path. The standard first statement grants the account root
`kms:*`, precisely to keep that door open.

Other things that come up: keys are **regional**, so a cross-region copy needs a key in the
destination region and a role allowed to use both (the most common cause of a silently failing
replication rule); **multi-Region keys** exist for the cases where the same key material must be
usable in several regions; **grants** are the mechanism AWS services use for temporary, scoped
delegation; and **aliases** let a key be rotated behind a stable name. Automatic rotation
rotates the backing material yearly while keeping the key id, so nothing in your configuration
changes.
