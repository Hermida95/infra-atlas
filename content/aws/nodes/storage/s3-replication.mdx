Replication copies objects from one bucket to another, automatically, after they are written.
Cross-Region Replication (CRR) is the one that appears in disaster-recovery and latency
questions; Same-Region Replication (SRR) is for log aggregation, compliance copies in another
account, and moving between storage classes or ownership.

It needs **versioning on both buckets** and an IAM role S3 assumes to do the copy. By default it
replicates only objects written **after** the rule is created -- existing objects need **Batch
Replication** to backfill, which is the step people forget. Delete markers can be replicated or
not, and deliberately not replicating them is a reasonable protection against an accidental mass
delete propagating.

**Replication Time Control (RTC)** is the part that turns it into an RPO. Without it,
replication is best-effort and usually seconds but with no promise. With it, 99.99% of objects
replicate within 15 minutes, there is an SLA, and you get CloudWatch metrics for pending bytes
and latency so you can alarm when it falls behind. If a requirement states a replication
objective in minutes, RTC is the answer.

Two other uses worth knowing. Replicating to a bucket in a **different account** gives you a
copy an attacker who compromises the source account cannot delete -- combine with Object Lock
for a real ransomware answer. And **two-way replication** keeps both regions writable for an
active/active design, at the cost of having to settle conflicts yourself, because S3 is
last-writer-wins and does not merge.

Encryption travels with it: replicating KMS-encrypted objects needs the key in the destination
region and the role granted use of both keys. That is the most common reason a replication rule
silently fails.
