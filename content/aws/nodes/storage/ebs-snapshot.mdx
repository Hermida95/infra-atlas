A snapshot is a point-in-time copy of a volume stored in S3 (in AWS's own buckets, not yours).
The first is effectively full; each later one stores only changed blocks, while still being
independently restorable -- deleting an old snapshot never breaks a newer one, because the
blocks it still needs are retained.

They are **regional** and that is what makes them a disaster-recovery tool: copying a snapshot
to another region, encrypted with a key that exists there, is the cheapest cross-region
protection for EBS data. It is also how an AMI travels, since an AMI is a manifest over
snapshots.

A few practical points. A snapshot of a running volume is **crash-consistent**, not
application-consistent: the database may have been mid-write. For anything transactional, flush
and freeze first, or use the service's own backup. Restoring creates a new volume whose blocks
are **lazily loaded** from S3, so the first read of each block is slow -- *Fast Snapshot
Restore* removes that at a price per snapshot per zone, which matters when the restore is part
of a recovery time objective you have promised.

Automate them with **Data Lifecycle Manager** or, better across an estate, **AWS Backup**, which
covers EBS alongside RDS, EFS, DynamoDB and the rest under one policy with cross-region and
cross-account copies. The **Recycle Bin** gives a retention window after a deletion, which is
the thing that saves you from a bad script. And watch for orphans: deregistering an AMI leaves
its snapshots behind, which is one of the quieter lines on a long-lived account's bill.
