QuickSight is the dashboard layer: it connects to Athena, Redshift, RDS, S3, OpenSearch and
others, and is priced **per user** -- authors by subscription and readers either by subscription
or pay-per-session -- rather than by server. That pricing model is usually why it is chosen over
running something else on EC2.

**SPICE** is its in-memory engine. Importing a dataset into SPICE makes dashboards fast and
stops every viewer's filter click becoming an Athena query that costs money; the alternative,
direct query, is always current but passes the cost and latency through. The usual pattern is
SPICE with a scheduled refresh for most dashboards, and direct query where freshness genuinely
matters.

Two features to know. **Row-level security** restricts rows by the signed-in user or group from
a rules dataset, so one dashboard serves many customers or regions without duplicating it. And
**embedded analytics** puts dashboards inside your own application with the session
authenticated by your identity, which is how a SaaS product ships customer-facing reporting
without building a charting stack.

The natural stack it completes: S3 for the data, Glue for the catalogue, Athena for the SQL,
QuickSight for the people -- entirely serverless, with no infrastructure between raw files and a
dashboard.
