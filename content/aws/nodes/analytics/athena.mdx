Athena runs SQL directly against files in S3. There is no cluster, no loading step and no
database: you define a table that says where the files are and what the columns mean, and query
it. Pricing is **per terabyte scanned**, which means every performance decision is also the cost
decision.

Three things reduce the bytes scanned, and together they routinely turn a $100 query into a $1
one. **Partitioning** -- writing data into `year=2026/month=11/day=05/` directories and
declaring those as partition keys -- lets a `WHERE` clause skip entire prefixes without reading
them. **Partition projection** is the version of that with no catalogue updates, which matters
once there are hundreds of thousands of partitions. **Columnar formats**, Parquet or ORC, mean a
query naming three columns reads three columns rather than whole rows. And **compression**
(Snappy, Zstd) reduces what is left. Converting a JSON log archive to partitioned Parquet is the
single highest-return thing most teams do to their data lake.

The table definitions live in the **Glue Data Catalog**, written by a crawler or declared in
DDL, and that catalogue is shared -- Redshift Spectrum, EMR and QuickSight read the same tables.
**Lake Formation** adds column- and row-level permissions over it, which is how a lake is shared
with analysts who may not see everything.

Practical features: **CTAS** (`CREATE TABLE AS SELECT`) is the usual way to convert and
repartition data in place; **workgroups** separate teams, set the result location and impose a
per-query data limit that stops an accidental full scan; **federated queries** reach into RDS,
DynamoDB and other sources through connectors; and **Athena for Spark** exists when SQL is not
enough.

Against Redshift: Athena for occasional, ad-hoc querying over a lake, with no infrastructure and
no cost when idle. Redshift for a warehouse queried continuously by many people, where indexes,
sort keys and materialised views pay off. The two coexist happily -- Redshift Spectrum queries
the same S3 data through the same catalogue.
