OpenSearch Service is managed OpenSearch (the Elasticsearch fork), used for two distinct jobs
that share an engine: **search** over documents, and **log and event analytics** with
dashboards.

For logs it is where you go when CloudWatch Logs Insights is not enough: full-text search across
sources, dashboards that update live, long interactive retention, and aggregations that a log
group cannot do. The usual ingestion path is Data Firehose, a CloudWatch Logs subscription
filter, or **OpenSearch Ingestion**, which is a managed Data Prepper pipeline.

The storage tiers are the cost story. **Hot** is attached storage for data being queried
constantly. **UltraWarm** keeps older indices in S3 with local caching at roughly a tenth of the
price, still searchable. **Cold** is cheaper again and must be attached before querying. An
index lifecycle policy that rolls indices through hot, UltraWarm and cold, then deletes them, is
what keeps a logging cluster affordable.

**OpenSearch Serverless** removes the node sizing question, with separate collection types for
time series, search and **vector** workloads -- that last one is why the service now appears in
generative-AI designs, as the vector store behind retrieval-augmented generation.

Operational notes that matter: a production domain runs across three Availability Zones with
dedicated master nodes, and the common failure is a cluster sized for storage but not for the
shard count -- too many small shards is the classic performance problem. Access control is
either IAM policies on the domain or **fine-grained access control** with its own users and
roles, which is what you need for per-index or per-document permissions.
