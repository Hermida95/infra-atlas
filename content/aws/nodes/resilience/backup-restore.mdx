The cheapest strategy, and for a great many workloads the correct one. Data is backed up and
copied to another region; nothing runs there until it is needed. Recovery means provisioning the
infrastructure and restoring the data.

**RPO** is the backup interval -- hours, typically. **RTO** is the restore plus the deploy, and
it is usually measured in hours too, dominated by how long it takes to restore a large database
rather than by anything else.

What makes it work is that the infrastructure is **code**. A disaster recovery plan whose first
step is "rebuild the VPC" is not a plan; one whose first step is "deploy the stack in the second
region" is. CloudFormation or Terraform, with StackSets for multi-account, plus AMIs and
container images already replicated, is the difference between hours and days.

The implementation is **AWS Backup** with cross-region and, importantly, **cross-account**
copies. The second of those is what makes it a security control as well as a DR one: a vault in
an account that no workload administrator can reach, with **Vault Lock in compliance mode**,
holds copies that an attacker with full admin in the source account cannot delete. That is the
honest answer to a ransomware requirement, and it is the only configuration where the answer is
genuinely "they cannot".

What it does not cover: an RTO in minutes, or an RPO in seconds. If the requirement states
either, this is the wrong row of the table and the question is testing whether you know that.
And the usual failure mode is never having tried it -- schedule restore tests, because a restore
that has never been performed is a hypothesis.
