Both regions serve real production traffic all the time. There is no failover, only the removal
of a region, so RTO approaches zero and the capacity you have been paying for is capacity you
are using.

It is also the hardest of the four to get right, and the difficulty is entirely in the data. The
moment two regions accept writes, **conflicts** become an application concern. DynamoDB **global
tables** resolve them last-writer-wins, which silently discards one update when two land in the
replication window -- fine when each item belongs to one user served by one region, wrong for a
shared counter or an inventory level. Aurora Global Database keeps a single writer, so one
region's writes cross the ocean, which bounds how active/active it really is. The designs that
work usually **partition ownership**: each user, tenant or shard has a home region that owns its
writes, and the others read.

Traffic is distributed by Route 53 **latency** or **weighted** records, or by Global Accelerator
with traffic dials. Health checks remove an unhealthy region automatically, and because both are
already live there is no promotion step.

Second-order problems worth knowing about: a **cell-based** design keeps one bad tenant inside
one cell rather than taking down a region; **idempotency** matters more because retries can land
in a different region; deployments have to be region-by-region with the ability to stop halfway;
and observability has to be aggregated or you are reading two of everything.

Choose it when the business genuinely cannot tolerate minutes of downtime, or when the latency
requirement already demands serving from several regions. Choose warm standby when it does not,
because active/active's real cost is not the second estate -- it is the permanent engineering
discipline required to keep two live regions consistent.
