A second region is a different problem from a second zone, not a bigger one. Within a region,
AWS gives you synchronous replication, a shared network and managed failover. Across regions you
get none of that for free, and three questions have to be answered explicitly.

**Where does the data live, and how does it get there?** Everything is asynchronous, so there is
an RPO greater than zero. Aurora Global Database replicates in under a second with one writer.
DynamoDB global tables are multi-writer with last-writer-wins. S3 replication is best-effort
unless you pay for Replication Time Control. Each of those is a different consistency model and
the application has to be correct under the one you pick.

**What decides which region is live?** Route 53 health checks with failover or latency records,
or Global Accelerator when DNS caching is unacceptable. Something has to make the call, and in
most real designs the honest answer is a human with a runbook, because automatic cross-region
failover can be worse than the outage.

**How does the second region exist at all?** Every piece of infrastructure must be deployable
there -- which means CloudFormation or Terraform and StackSets, not a console. AMIs, container
images, KMS keys, certificates, secrets and quotas are all regional and all have to be
replicated or recreated. The quota one bites hardest: a failover that needs 200 instances in a
region where the account is limited to 20 does not happen.

Beyond disaster recovery there are two other reasons to go multi-region, and they ask different
things. **Latency** for a global audience, which may be satisfied by CloudFront or Global
Accelerator without moving the application at all. And **data residency**, where the requirement
is that certain data never leaves a jurisdiction -- which is a partitioning problem rather than
a replication one.

The honest advice: most workloads should be excellent at multi-AZ before they are mediocre at
multi-region. A second region doubles the operational surface, and an untested one provides
false confidence rather than availability.
