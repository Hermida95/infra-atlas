Every application in a migration gets one of seven dispositions, and a Professional-level
question is usually asking you to choose the right one for a described constraint.

**Retire** first, always. Discovery consistently finds a tenth to a fifth of servers that
nothing talks to. Not migrating them is free. **Retain** is the honest answer for anything
blocked by a dependency, a licence, a compliance boundary or a decommissioning date that is
sooner than the migration.

**Relocate** moves VMware workloads to VMware Cloud on AWS without converting them -- fastest of
all, and it changes nothing about the application. **Rehost** is the default for the bulk: a
lift and shift into EC2, usually with **MGN**, where the operating system and application are
unchanged and only the hardware underneath is different. It is fast, low risk, and it is what
makes the data-centre exit date achievable; the optimisation happens afterwards, in the cloud,
where it is easier.

**Replatform** changes something without changing the architecture: the same application onto
**RDS** instead of a self-managed database, or into containers, or onto a managed message
broker. **Repurchase** replaces it with SaaS. **Refactor** rewrites it -- the largest benefit
and the largest cost, and the one that should follow a migration rather than block it.

The decision needs evidence, which is what **Migration Hub** and discovery agents are for:
utilisation (which drives right-sizing), dependencies (which drive the move groups -- things
that talk to each other move together), and licences. A plan built on a spreadsheet of server
names and a hope is the usual failure mode.

The sequencing advice that comes up repeatedly: rehost the majority to hit the deadline, pick a
small number of high-value applications to replatform during the move, and schedule refactoring
for after. Trying to refactor everything during a migration is how data-centre leases get
extended.
