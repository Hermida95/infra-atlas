Elastic Disaster Recovery is MGN's replication engine kept running permanently. An agent on each
source server -- on-premises, in another cloud, or in another AWS region -- replicates disks
continuously into a low-cost staging area in your recovery account, using small staging
instances and cheap storage. Nothing is running as a full-size instance until you need it.

That gives an **RPO measured in seconds** and an **RTO of minutes**, for servers you have not
rebuilt as infrastructure-as-code. That last clause is the point: the cloud-native answer to
disaster recovery is templates plus replicated data, but a lifted-and-shifted estate of two
hundred Windows and Linux servers is not described in templates, and will not be for years. DRS
covers exactly that gap.

Recovery launches real EC2 instances from the replicated volumes, with launch templates deciding
type, subnet and security groups. Crucially, you can run a **drill** at any time into an
isolated subnet without affecting replication or the source -- which makes a tested DR plan
practical rather than theoretical. **Point-in-time recovery** from the staging snapshots also
means it covers ransomware and corruption, not just infrastructure failure.

Where it sits: it is the **pilot light** strategy, implemented for servers. Against MGN, the
engine is the same and the intent is opposite -- MGN is a one-way move with a cutover, DRS is a
standing capability you hope never to use. Against AWS Backup, DRS gives seconds of RPO and
minutes of RTO, where backup and restore gives hours of both.
