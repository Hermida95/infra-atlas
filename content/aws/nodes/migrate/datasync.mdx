DataSync is a managed transfer service for file and object data. It moves between on-premises
storage (NFS, SMB, HDFS, self-managed object stores) and AWS (S3, EFS, FSx), and between AWS
services, at up to about ten times the speed of open-source tools thanks to a purpose-built
protocol with parallel transfers and compression.

What you get over `rsync` or a script: **incremental** transfers that only move what changed,
end-to-end **validation** of every file, **scheduling**, bandwidth throttling so it does not
saturate the link during business hours, filtering by pattern, preservation of metadata and
permissions, and CloudWatch metrics and logging for each task.

It needs an **agent** -- a VM on-premises, or an EC2 instance for in-cloud transfers -- except
for AWS-to-AWS transfers, which are agentless.

Where it sits among the neighbours. **Storage Gateway** is a standing presence that presents a
protocol locally and keeps data in AWS; DataSync is a transfer, one-off or scheduled.
**Snowball** is what you use when the arithmetic says the network cannot finish in time -- and
that arithmetic is worth doing explicitly, because a 1 Gbps link moves roughly 10 TB a day at
best. **S3 Transfer Acceleration** is for uploads to S3 over long distances from many clients,
not for bulk migration.

The common pairing: DataSync over Direct Connect for the initial bulk and ongoing sync, with a
final incremental run at cutover. For very large estates, Snowball for the bulk and DataSync for
the delta afterwards.
