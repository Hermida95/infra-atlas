Migration Hub is the inventory and the progress view. It gathers data from **Application
Discovery Service** -- an agent on each server, or an agentless appliance for VMware -- and
tracks the status of each application as the migration services do their work.

The discovery data is what turns a plan from a guess into a decision. **Utilisation** across
CPU, memory, disk and network gives you right-sizing rather than like-for-like, which is usually
where the first cloud saving is. **Network dependencies** -- who talks to whom, on which ports
-- define the **move groups**, because applications that talk to each other should move together
or you are left with chatty traffic crossing a VPN. And the inventory surfaces the servers
nobody can account for, which is the Retire list.

**Migration Hub Strategy Recommendations** goes a step further and analyses the running
application -- the runtime, the framework, the database -- to suggest one of the 7 Rs per
component with an estimated effort. It is a starting point for the conversation, not an answer.

**Refactor Spaces** is the other piece worth knowing by name: it sets up the routing and account
structure for the **strangler fig** pattern, where new functionality is built as separate
services in front of a legacy application and traffic is moved route by route, so a monolith is
replaced incrementally rather than in one cutover.

Progress tracking is the mundane part that matters at scale: with two hundred servers in waves,
one place showing replication status, cutover readiness and what has already moved is the
difference between a programme and a spreadsheet.
