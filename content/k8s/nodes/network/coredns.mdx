CoreDNS runs as a small Deployment in `kube-system`, fronted by a ClusterIP that every Pod's
`/etc/resolv.conf` points at. It answers cluster names from its own watch of Services and
EndpointSlices, and forwards everything else to the node's upstream resolvers. It is the first
thing in the path of every service-to-service call, which gives it an outsized influence on
tail latency.

**The `ndots: 5` behaviour is the classic performance trap.** A Pod's resolv.conf has a search
list -- `<namespace>.svc.cluster.local`, `svc.cluster.local`, `cluster.local`, plus the node's
domains -- and `ndots: 5` means any name with fewer than five dots is tried with each suffix
appended *before* being tried as written. So resolving `api.example.com` performs four or five
failed lookups first, on every call, from every Pod. The fixes: a trailing dot
(`api.example.com.`) to make it fully qualified, a `dnsConfig` with a lower `ndots` on the Pod,
or NodeLocal DNSCache so the failures are answered locally.

**Its failure is total and looks like something else.** When DNS is slow or unavailable, every
application reports connection errors to every dependency, and nobody's dashboard says "DNS".
The usual causes are too few replicas for the request rate, CPU throttling on the CoreDNS Pods
(a CPU limit on an infrastructure component that handles thousands of queries a second is a bad
idea), and a default-deny NetworkPolicy that forgot to allow UDP 53 egress to kube-system --
which produces an entire namespace that cannot resolve anything, immediately after a security
change.

**At scale, run NodeLocal DNSCache.** A DaemonSet caching on each node answers most queries
without leaving the host, absorbs the search-list churn, and converts the UDP conntrack pressure
that causes intermittent five-second timeouts into local hits. It is one of the highest-value
additions to a busy cluster.

**Two Pod-level fields are worth knowing.** `dnsPolicy: ClusterFirst` is the default;
`None` with an explicit `dnsConfig` is how you take full control for a workload with unusual
needs, and `hostNetwork` Pods need `dnsPolicy: ClusterFirstWithHostNet` or they will not use
cluster DNS at all.
