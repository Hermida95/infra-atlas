An EndpointSlice is the resolved answer to a Service's selector: a list of Pod addresses, their
ports, their readiness, and the node and zone each one is on. A controller in the
controller-manager maintains them, and every kube-proxy and every mesh sidecar in the cluster
watches them to program its own forwarding.

**It replaced the Endpoints object for a scaling reason.** The old design put every address for a
Service into one object, so a Service with 5,000 Pods had one very large object, and a single Pod
becoming ready rewrote all of it -- and that rewrite was then sent to every node watching. Slices
cap at 100 endpoints each, so one Pod change rewrites one small object. On big clusters this was
the difference between a control plane that worked and one that spent its life distributing
endpoint updates.

**It is the right place to look when traffic is wrong.** `kubectl get endpointslices -l
kubernetes.io/service-name=<svc>` answers the question "does this Service know about any Pods",
which separates an application problem from a wiring problem instantly. Empty means the selector,
the labels or readiness; populated but with the wrong port means `targetPort`; populated and
correct means the problem is further along -- kube-proxy, policy, or the application itself.

**The three conditions encode a subtlety that matters during deploys.** `ready` follows the
readiness probe. `serving` is like ready but stays true for a terminating Pod, and
`terminating` marks a Pod being deleted. A proxy that understands all three can keep sending
in-flight work to a draining Pod while routing new work elsewhere; kube-proxy's simpler
behaviour -- remove it from the rules -- is why `preStop` sleeps are still needed.

**Topology fields enable same-zone routing**, which is not a performance nicety but a cost one:
cross-zone traffic is billed in every major cloud. `trafficDistribution: PreferClose` on the
Service uses this to keep traffic local when there is a healthy local endpoint and fall back when
there is not, which is strictly better than the old `Local` traffic policy that just blackholed.
