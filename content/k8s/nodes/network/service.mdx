A Service solves one problem: Pod IPs change constantly and nothing should have to track them.
It holds a label selector, a controller keeps the matching ready Pods in EndpointSlices, and the
Service gets a stable virtual IP and a DNS name that front that list. The indirection is what
makes rolling updates, scaling and node failures invisible to callers.

**Nothing is listening on a ClusterIP.** This is the detail that makes Kubernetes networking
click. The address is virtual: it exists only as rules in every node's iptables, nftables or IPVS
tables, programmed by kube-proxy, that rewrite the destination of outgoing packets to one of the
Pod IPs. There is no proxy process in the path, no single point of failure, and no latency hop --
and also no way to ping a ClusterIP or run `tcpdump` on it, which confuses people debugging for
the first time.

**The failure everyone meets first is a Service with no endpoints.** `kubectl get endpointslices`
showing nothing means the selector matches no ready Pods, and there are only three causes:
the labels do not match the selector, the Pods are not Ready, or the Pods are not there.
`kubectl get pods --show-labels` next to `kubectl describe svc` resolves it in seconds. The
second most common is a `targetPort` that does not match the container's actual port -- the
Service exists, has endpoints, and connects to a closed port.

**`port` and `targetPort` are different things** and naming the target is the better habit:
`targetPort: http` resolves against the container's named port, so changing the application's
listening port is one edit in the Deployment rather than two in two objects.

**Traffic policies decide whether locality matters.**
`internalTrafficPolicy: Local` and `externalTrafficPolicy: Local` keep traffic on the receiving
node -- which preserves the client IP and avoids a cross-zone hop, and silently blackholes
traffic arriving at a node with no local Pod. Topology-aware routing is the gentler version of
the same idea, preferring same-zone endpoints while still falling back.
