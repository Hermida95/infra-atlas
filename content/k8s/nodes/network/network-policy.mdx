By default every Pod in a cluster can talk to every other Pod, in any namespace. A NetworkPolicy
changes that for the Pods it selects: once a Pod is selected by any policy of a given type, all
traffic of that type is denied except what some policy allows. Policies are purely additive --
there is no deny rule and no ordering -- so the model is "nothing selects you and everything is
allowed, or something selects you and only the union of allowances applies".

**The standard starting point is a default-deny per namespace:** a policy with an empty
`podSelector` and both policy types, allowing nothing. Then add allowances per workload. Doing it
the other way round -- allow rules without a default-deny -- feels productive and protects
nothing, because unselected Pods are still wide open.

**DNS is the first thing a default-deny breaks**, and it breaks everything at once. Egress to UDP
and TCP port 53 towards kube-system must be allowed explicitly, or no Pod in the namespace can
resolve a name, and the symptom is every application reporting that every dependency is
unreachable. It is the single most common incident caused by a NetworkPolicy rollout.

**The `from` block's AND/OR rule is the syntax trap.** Inside one element, a
`namespaceSelector` and a `podSelector` together mean "Pods matching this selector *in*
namespaces matching that one". As two separate elements they mean "any Pod in those namespaces,
or any Pod matching that selector in my namespace". The two YAMLs differ by two characters of
indentation and say completely different things, with no feedback either way.

**What it cannot do.** It is L3/L4: no HTTP paths, no methods, no hostnames -- an `ipBlock`
cannot usefully name `api.stripe.com`, whose addresses change. It does not apply to
`hostNetwork` Pods. It cannot express "deny". And egress policies interact badly with
Services, since policy is evaluated on the translated Pod addresses rather than on the ClusterIP
you wrote in your head. For named-host egress, L7 rules or identity-based policy, the answer is
a CNI with extensions (Cilium) or a mesh -- with NetworkPolicy still underneath as the floor
that applies even when the sidecar is missing.
