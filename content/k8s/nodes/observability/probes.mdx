Probes are health checks the kubelet runs against a container. There are three, they have
completely different consequences, and almost every probe-related incident comes from using the
wrong one.

**Readiness controls traffic.** When it fails, the Pod is taken out of its Service's endpoints and
stops receiving requests -- and nothing else happens. That makes it the right place to express
"I cannot serve right now": a dependency is down, a cache is warming, the queue backlog is too
deep. It is also what a rolling update waits on, which makes it the only thing standing between a
broken new version and all your replicas.

**Liveness controls restarts, and it should be used sparingly.** When it fails past its threshold,
the container is killed. The only conditions it should detect are those a restart genuinely
fixes -- a deadlock, a wedged event loop. Pointing a liveness probe at an endpoint that checks
the database is the classic self-inflicted outage: the database has a bad minute, every replica
fails liveness, every replica restarts simultaneously, and now you have a cold fleet hammering a
struggling database. **Liveness should never check a dependency.**

**Startup probes exist because the other two have one set of timings.** A JVM that takes ninety
seconds to start needs either a liveness `failureThreshold` high enough to cover it -- which then
also applies forever, making real failures slow to detect -- or a startup probe, which runs with
its own generous budget and hands over to the normal probes once it passes. Any slow-starting
application should have one.

**The handler choice matters more than the timings.** A `tcpSocket` probe succeeds the moment the
port is bound, which for most frameworks is before the application can answer anything -- so it
reports ready far too early. An `httpGet` to a real endpoint is what you want, and the endpoint
should check the things the Pod needs to serve *this* request, not the health of the whole
system. Two endpoints, `/healthz` for liveness (is the process sane) and `/readyz` for readiness
(can I serve), is the pattern worth implementing.

**Having no readiness probe is the most expensive default.** A Pod with none is Ready as soon as
the container starts, so it receives traffic before it can serve it, and a rollout completes
regardless of whether the new version works at all.
