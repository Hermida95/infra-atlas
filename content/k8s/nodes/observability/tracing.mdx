A trace is one request's path through a system: a tree of spans, each with a start, a duration,
attributes, and a parent. In a cluster where a single page view touches six services, it is the
only thing that answers "which of them was slow", and the only thing that shows that the answer
was "the fourth one called the third one eleven times".

**Context propagation is the whole requirement, and it is where adoption fails.** Every service
must read the incoming `traceparent` header and pass it on. One service in the middle that does
not forward it breaks the trace into two unrelated fragments, and the gap is invisible from either
end. This is why tracing is an all-or-nothing adoption per request path, and why the first
investment is a shared middleware or library rather than a backend.

**OpenTelemetry is the answer to the instrumentation question.** One vendor-neutral SDK per
language, auto-instrumentation for the common frameworks and HTTP clients, and the OTel Collector
as a DaemonSet or Deployment that receives, batches, samples and exports to whatever backend --
Jaeger, Tempo, a managed APM. Having the Collector in the middle is what makes the backend choice
reversible.

**Sampling is a decision you cannot avoid.** Tracing every request is expensive in network and
storage; head sampling (decide at the start, keep 1%) is cheap and systematically throws away the
interesting requests, because errors and slow requests are rare. Tail sampling in the Collector
-- buffer the spans, then keep the trace if it contains an error or exceeded a latency threshold
-- is what makes traces useful in production, and it is the setting worth the configuration
effort.

**What a mesh gives you and what it does not.** Sidecar proxies produce a span per network hop
with no application change, which maps the topology and shows per-hop latency. They cannot see a
slow database query, a lock, or a loop inside a handler -- the places the time actually goes. The
practical combination is mesh spans for the edges and application instrumentation for the
inside, joined by the same trace id, with that id also in every log line.
