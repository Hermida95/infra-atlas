Two copies of a controller running at once is not redundancy, it is a race: both read the same
spec, both decide the same work is missing, both do it. So controllers that cannot be sharded
elect a leader, and the losers wait.

**The mechanism is deliberately boring.** A `Lease` object in `coordination.k8s.io` holds the
current holder's identity and a renewal timestamp. The holder updates it every few seconds
(`renewDeadline`); a candidate that sees the lease unrenewed for longer than `leaseDuration`
takes it over by writing its own name. Optimistic concurrency on the object makes the takeover
atomic -- two candidates writing at once, one gets a conflict and backs off. `kubectl -n
kube-system get leases` shows who currently holds the scheduler and the controller-manager.

**The timings are a trade-off you can see in an incident.** Short durations mean fast failover
and more API writes, plus a real risk of a healthy-but-slow leader losing its lease during an
API server hiccup and causing a needless handover. The defaults -- around fifteen seconds of
lease with a ten-second renewal deadline -- mean a crashed controller leaves the cluster without
that loop for up to fifteen seconds. For most controllers that is invisible; for one driving
traffic it may not be.

**It is not fencing.** A leader that is partitioned from the API server but still able to reach
the thing it manages can keep acting after its lease has been taken: Kubernetes leases give you
mutual exclusion against cooperating participants, not a guarantee against a split brain. A
controller doing something genuinely dangerous -- mounting a volume, promoting a database --
needs its own fencing at that resource, which is exactly why storage attachment is handled by
the attach-detach controller and CSI rather than by whoever thinks they are the leader.

If you write an operator with controller-runtime, this is one flag
(`--leader-elect`) and a lease name. Turning it on before the second replica, not after the
first duplicate-resource incident, is the cheap order.
