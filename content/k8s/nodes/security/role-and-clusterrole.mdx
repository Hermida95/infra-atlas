A Role is a namespaced list of rules; a ClusterRole is the same list, cluster-scoped. Neither
grants anything. The scope difference is about what the rules can address: cluster-scoped
resources -- Nodes, PersistentVolumes, Namespaces, CustomResourceDefinitions, ClusterRoles
themselves -- can only appear in a ClusterRole, because a namespaced Role has no way to refer to
an object with no namespace.

**The four built-in ClusterRoles are worth using rather than reinventing.**
`view` is read-only excluding Secrets, `edit` is read-write on workloads but cannot change RBAC,
`admin` adds RBAC within the namespace, and `cluster-admin` is everything everywhere. Bound with
a *RoleBinding*, the first three give a team real autonomy inside its namespace with no
cluster-wide reach -- which covers most of what people write custom roles for.

**The empty `apiGroups` entry is the classic first mistake.** Pods, Services, ConfigMaps, Secrets
and Nodes are in the core group, written as `""`. Deployments are in `apps`, Ingresses in
`networking.k8s.io`, Roles in `rbac.authorization.k8s.io`. A rule listing
`resources: ["deployments"]` under `apiGroups: [""]` is syntactically fine and matches nothing.
`kubectl api-resources` prints the right group for everything the cluster serves.

**Subresources are separate and often forgotten.** `pods` does not include `pods/log`,
`pods/exec`, `pods/portforward` or `pods/status`. A debugging role needs `pods/log` explicitly,
and *not* granting `pods/exec` is often the whole point -- a shell in a Pod is equivalent to
whatever that Pod can reach, including its ServiceAccount token.

**`resourceNames` is narrower than it looks.** It restricts rules for verbs that address one
object (`get`, `update`, `patch`, `delete`) and has no effect on `list` or `watch`, which address a
collection. So "may read only this ConfigMap" works for `get` and cannot prevent listing all of
them. When that matters, the answer is a separate namespace, not a cleverer role.
