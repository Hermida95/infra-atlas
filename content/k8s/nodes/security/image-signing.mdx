Signing an image produces a cryptographic statement that a specific digest was built and approved
by a specific identity. Sigstore and its `cosign` tool made this practical: keyless signing uses
an OIDC identity (a GitHub Actions workflow, say) to obtain a short-lived certificate, signs the
digest, and records the signature in a public transparency log -- so there is no long-lived
signing key to protect.

**A signature that nobody verifies is decoration.** The whole value is in the enforcement point:
an admission policy that rejects any Pod whose image is not signed by the expected identity.
Kyverno's `verifyImages` rule and Gatekeeper with the `cosign` provider both do this, and both
also *mutate* the image reference to its digest, which closes the gap where a verified tag could
later point elsewhere. Without that, you have a supply chain claim and a cluster that ignores it.

**Which is why digests matter so much here.** A signature is over a digest. A Pod spec saying
`app:1.4.2` resolves to whatever that tag points at *when the kubelet pulls it* -- potentially
after verification happened, on a different node, to a different image. Deploying by digest, or
having the admission controller rewrite to a digest, is what makes the chain unbroken.

**Provenance and SBOMs are the next layer and attach the same way.** A SLSA provenance attestation
says which source commit, which builder and which parameters produced the digest; an SBOM lists
what is inside it. Both are stored alongside the image in the registry as attestations, both are
signed, and both can be required at admission -- "this image must have provenance from our
builder, and its SBOM must contain no package with a critical CVE" is expressible today.

**The realistic adoption path** is: sign in CI (one step, keyless), require signatures in staging
with a policy in audit mode, verify the identity rather than merely the presence of a signature,
then enforce in production per namespace. The step people skip is checking the *identity* -- a
policy that accepts any valid signature accepts an attacker's signature too.
