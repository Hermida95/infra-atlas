When a node is running out of memory, disk or inodes, the kubelet starts deleting Pods on it.
This is deliberate and much better than the alternative: a kernel out-of-memory kill takes an
arbitrary process at an arbitrary moment, possibly the kubelet or the container runtime, leaving
the node unusable. Eviction gets ahead of it, picks a victim on purpose, and reports a Pod status
the control plane can act on by rescheduling elsewhere.

**Thresholds are configured on the kubelet, not in the cluster.** `memory.available`,
`nodefs.available`, `imagefs.available` and their inode counterparts each have a *hard*
threshold (act immediately) and a *soft* threshold (act after a grace period, honouring
`terminationGracePeriod`). Defaults are modest -- 100Mi of memory, 10% of disk -- and the
`--eviction-minimum-reclaim` and `--kube-reserved` flags are what stop the node from
oscillating in and out of pressure.

**Victim selection follows a fixed order.** First, Pods whose usage exceeds their requests, with
BestEffort Pods (no requests at all) effectively always in that set; among those, the ones most
over request by priority order. Guaranteed Pods -- requests equal to limits -- are evicted only
when there is nothing else left. So declaring honest requests is not bureaucracy, it is the
thing that decides whether your Pod survives a bad neighbour.

**Three traps worth knowing.** First, eviction here ignores PodDisruptionBudgets: those govern
the voluntary eviction API used by `kubectl drain`, and a node under pressure is not negotiating.
Second, a Pod evicted for disk often comes back and fills the disk again, so the real fix is
usually `emptyDir` `sizeLimit`, log rotation, or an ephemeral-storage request that makes the
scheduler account for it. Third, memory-backed `emptyDir` counts against the Pod's memory limit,
which surprises people writing large files to `/tmp` in a tmpfs.

Operationally, `kubectl get events --field-selector reason=Evicted` and the
`MemoryPressure` / `DiskPressure` node conditions are the trail. A cluster evicting regularly is
a cluster whose requests are wrong, not one that needs higher thresholds.
