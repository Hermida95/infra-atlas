Everything in Kubernetes is an object: a record in one database, served by one HTTP API, with
the same five fields. There are no special cases -- a Pod, a ClusterRole and a CRD you wrote
yesterday are stored, validated, versioned and watched by exactly the same machinery. This is
why the system is learnable: the shape is paid for once.

**Spec and status are the whole model.** `spec` is desired state, written by you. `status` is
observed state, written by the controller that owns the object. You never write status and the
controller never writes spec, and that one rule is what makes the system declarative rather
than a remote-procedure call. `kubectl get -o yaml` on anything shows the two side by side, and
the difference between them is the answer to "why is this not working yet".

**Names are scoped and immutable.** A name is unique for a kind inside a namespace, and you
cannot rename an object -- you delete it and create another, which for a Pod means a new
container and for a PersistentVolumeClaim can mean losing a volume. `generateName` exists for
when you want the server to pick. Cluster-scoped kinds (Node, PersistentVolume, ClusterRole)
have no namespace at all, which is also why a RoleBinding cannot grant access to them.

**metadata is where the cluster keeps its own notes.** `ownerReferences` makes garbage
collection work, `finalizers` block deletion until some cleanup runs, `resourceVersion` makes
concurrent writes safe, and `managedFields` records who owns which field. None of it is about
what your application does, and all of it explains behaviour that otherwise looks like magic:
why deleting a Deployment deletes its Pods, why a namespace sits in `Terminating` forever, why
your `kubectl apply` undid someone's change.

The practical habit is to read any new resource by asking the same three questions: what does
its spec let me ask for, what does its status tell me, and which controller is watching it. If
nothing is watching it, nothing will ever happen -- which is exactly what a CRD without its
operator installed looks like.
