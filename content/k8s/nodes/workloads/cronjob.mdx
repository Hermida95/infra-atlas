A CronJob holds a cron expression and a Job template, and creates a Job each time the schedule
fires. Everything about how that work runs -- retries, parallelism, timeouts, cleanup -- belongs
to the Job, so a CronJob is a thin scheduler on top of the previous object and should be read
that way.

**Time zone and clock.** The schedule is interpreted in UTC unless you set `spec.timeZone`, which
is a newer field and the only correct way to do it -- the old workaround of shifting the
expression breaks twice a year with daylight saving. The firing itself is driven by the
controller-manager, so a control plane that was down over a scheduled time simply misses it; if
the miss window exceeds `startingDeadlineSeconds` the run is skipped entirely and recorded as a
`MissedSchedule` event.

**`concurrencyPolicy` is the field to decide deliberately.** `Allow` (the default) lets a new Job
start while the previous one is still running, which for a report that takes eleven minutes on a
ten-minute schedule means ever-growing overlap. `Forbid` skips the new run. `Replace` kills the
old one and starts fresh. Picking the default by accident is how a nightly export ends up with
six copies competing for the same rows.

**At-least-once, not exactly-once.** The controller can create two Jobs for one schedule in rare
cases, and a Job can run a Pod more than once on retry. So anything a CronJob does should be
idempotent, or guarded by its own lock -- a row in a database, an advisory lock, a lease object.
Treating a CronJob as a guarantee of singularity is the mistake behind most duplicated-email
incidents.

**History limits are not optional in practice.** `successfulJobsHistoryLimit` (3) and
`failedJobsHistoryLimit` (1) keep a useful trail; combined with `ttlSecondsAfterFinished` on the
Job template they keep the object count bounded. And `suspend: true` is the right way to stop a
misbehaving schedule during an incident -- it leaves the object in place, so the change is
visible in git rather than being a deletion someone has to remember to undo.
