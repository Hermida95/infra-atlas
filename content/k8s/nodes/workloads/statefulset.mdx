A StatefulSet runs Pods with identity. Each one gets a predictable name, its own persistent
volume that follows it, a DNS record of its own, and a guaranteed order for creation, update and
deletion. Those are precisely the requirements of software that forms a cluster -- Kafka,
Cassandra, etcd, Elasticsearch, PostgreSQL with replication -- where "replica 2" is a specific
member with specific data and not an interchangeable copy.

**Storage is the part with the sharpest edges.** `volumeClaimTemplates` creates one PVC per Pod,
named after it, and reattaches it when the Pod is rescheduled. Those PVCs are *not* deleted when
the StatefulSet is deleted, which is a deliberate safety choice and a recurring surprise:
deleting and recreating a StatefulSet reuses the old data, and scaling down from 5 to 3 leaves
two PVCs behind, still billed, waiting for a scale-up. `persistentVolumeClaimRetentionPolicy`
now lets you choose, and it is worth setting explicitly rather than discovering the default.

**Zone pinning follows from storage.** A PVC bound to a block volume in one availability zone can
only be mounted in that zone, so `web-1` is pinned there for life. `WaitForFirstConsumer` on the
StorageClass makes the volume follow the scheduler's choice rather than the other way round,
which is the difference between a StatefulSet that spreads across zones and one that cannot
schedule after a zone fails.

**Ordering is a double-edged default.** It makes bootstrapping a quorum possible and makes
rolling updates safe, with `partition` in the update strategy allowing a genuine canary -- update
only ordinals above N, verify, then lower the partition. It also means one Pod that will not
become Ready blocks the entire rollout, indefinitely, with no progress deadline to rescue you.
`podManagementPolicy: Parallel` removes the ordering for startup and scaling, not for updates.

**A StatefulSet is not a database operator.** It gives you identity and storage; it knows nothing
about electing a leader, taking a backup, promoting a replica or running a restore. For anything
you would be paged about, an operator (CloudNativePG, the Strimzi Kafka operator) or a managed
service outside the cluster is the serious answer.
