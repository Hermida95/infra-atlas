A rolling update replaces Pods incrementally: bring up some new ones, wait for them to be Ready,
take down some old ones, repeat. The two numbers that control it are `maxSurge` (how far above
the replica count you may go) and `maxUnavailable` (how far below). Setting `maxUnavailable: 0`
with `maxSurge: 1` is the conservative shape -- never less capacity than you asked for, one
extra Pod at a time -- and it needs room in the cluster to work.

**"Rolling update" and "zero downtime" are not the same claim**, and the gap is where most
deploy-time errors come from. Three things have to be true as well. First, a readiness probe that
is only satisfied when the application can actually serve -- otherwise new Pods take traffic
before they are ready. Second, a shutdown sequence that drains: when a Pod is deleted, endpoint
removal and `SIGTERM` happen concurrently, and the endpoint change takes time to reach every
node's kube-proxy, so a Pod that exits promptly on SIGTERM will refuse requests that were already
in flight. Third, a `terminationGracePeriodSeconds` long enough to finish them.

**The standard fix for the second point is a `preStop` hook that sleeps** for a few seconds: the
container keeps serving while the endpoint deletion propagates, then exits cleanly. It feels like
a hack and it is the documented answer; `sleep 5` plus a grace period of 30 removes almost all
502s during deploys. The alternative -- having the app handle SIGTERM by failing readiness,
waiting, then draining -- is better and requires application changes.

**`minReadySeconds` is underused.** With it at zero, a Pod that becomes Ready and crashes two
seconds later has already let the rollout advance, so a bad version can march through every
replica. Ten or fifteen seconds costs a slower deploy and catches the class of failure that
happens on first real traffic.

**And `Recreate` has one legitimate use**: a workload that cannot have two versions running at
once -- an incompatible schema, a single-writer lock. It accepts downtime explicitly, which is
better than pretending a rolling update is safe for it.
