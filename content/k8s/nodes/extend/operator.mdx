An operator is a controller plus one or more custom resources, packaging the operational
knowledge for a piece of software. The canonical example is a database: a
`PostgresCluster` object describes what you want, and the operator handles provisioning,
replication, failover, backups, restores, connection pooling and minor version upgrades --
the things a StatefulSet knows nothing about.

**It is the honest answer to "can I run a database on Kubernetes".** A StatefulSet gives you
stable names and volumes, and leaves every interesting failure to you: which replica is primary,
how a failover is decided, how a backup is taken consistently, how a restore works, how a major
version upgrade proceeds. A mature operator -- CloudNativePG, the Strimzi Kafka operator,
Crunchy, Vitess -- encodes answers that have been tested by people who were paged for them.

**Writing one is less work than it used to be, and the hard part is not the code.**
`controller-runtime` with Kubebuilder or the Operator SDK generates the scaffolding and gives you
a reconcile function. The difficulty is semantics: an idempotent reconcile (it will run again
with nothing to do), honest status conditions with `observedGeneration`, finalizers for external
cleanup, and never assuming you are the only writer. A reconcile that creates without checking,
or that treats its own status write as a trigger, produces an infinite loop -- the classic first
operator bug.

**Installing someone else's is a trust decision.** Operators typically request a ClusterRole over
the kinds they manage, often including Secrets, and sometimes cluster-wide. That is a component
with wide permissions, in the critical path of your data, from a third party. Reading the
ClusterRole in the chart before applying it, scoping the operator to specific namespaces where
it supports that, and preferring projects with real maintenance are the proportionate responses.

**The uninstall path is the one people discover too late.** Removing an operator while its custom
resources still exist leaves objects with finalizers nobody will clear, which blocks namespace
deletion. The correct order is always: delete the resources, let the operator clean up, then
remove the operator and its CRDs.
